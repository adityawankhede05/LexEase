--- 1. GENERAL MODEL ARCHITECTURE ---
  * Model Repository:  AnkushRaheja/Cls_Class_Risk_Scr ('Legal AI Risk Analyzer V7')
  * Base Encoder:      nlpaueb/legal-bert-base-uncased
  * Multi-task design: Yes. The model consists of a shared Legal-BERT encoder followed by:
     1. A Classification Head: Sequential(Linear(768->384), ReLU, Dropout, Linear(384->100)) predicting 100 clause categories.
     2. A Regression Head:     Sequential(Linear(768->384), ReLU, Dropout, Linear(384->1), Sigmoid) predicting a continuous risk score.
  * Separability:      Clause type and risk are separate tasks computed by different heads over the shared representation.
  * Hard-coded rules:  None in the model architecture itself; however, the classification to LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH in post-processing relies on thresholds.

--- 4. THRESHOLD OPTIMIZATION ANALYSIS ---
  * Official thresholds (score < 40 = LOW, score < 70 = MEDIUM, score >= 70 = HIGH):
     - Under these thresholds, the accuracy is 30.67% and the model predicts HIGH only 1 time.
     - Reason: The raw scores are extremely concentrated in the range [30.00, 55.00]. Only one clause scored >= 70.00.
  * Optimized thresholds (found via grid search on validation set):
     - Optimal Threshold 1 (LOW -> MEDIUM):  54.00
     - Optimal Threshold 2 (MEDIUM -> HIGH): 63.00
     - Optimized Accuracy:                  62.0000%

--- 5. ROOT CAUSE DIAGNOSIS & CONCLUSION ---
1. Output of Pretrained Model:
   - The model outputs a continuous value between 0.0 and 1.0 representing the predicted risk level of the clause.
2. Meaning of Raw Scores:
   - The raw scores represent the sigmoid activation of the regression task. They do not represent a probability, but rather a relative scale of risk.
3. Why is it predicting HIGH only once?
   - Incorrect Thresholding Mismatch: The official thresholds (40 and 70) assume the model's activations are evenly distributed. On our real-world dataset, the model's scores are compressed, which causes the threshold of 70 to be virtually unreachable.
4. Suitability for Dedicated Risk Model:
   - Yes, this model is technically suitable as a foundation because the continuous risk regression head correlates with risk severity better than our categorical classifiers, but the post-processing thresholds must be calibrated.
