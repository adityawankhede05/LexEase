--- 3. DETAILED RECOMMENDATIONS FOR EXPERIMENT 5 ---
A. Is the existing dataset suitable for context-aware risk training?
   - No. Out of the 41 clause types, only 6 are represented by multiple risk levels with less than 80% correlation. For 35 out of 41 clause types, risk is virtually a proxy for the clause type label itself. The model cannot learn contextual risk when there is no risk variation within the clause types during training.

B. Which clause types should be used for Experiment 5?
   - We should focus exclusively on the 6 multi-risk clause types that show variance:
     * Affiliate License-Licensee (Support: 32)
     * Affiliate License-Licensor (Support: 19)
     * Cap On Liability (Support: 436)
     * Competitive Restriction Exception (Support: 75)
     * Covenant Not To Sue (Support: 127)
     * Effective Date (Support: 123)
     * Exclusivity (Support: 314)
     * Expiration Date (Support: 332)
     * Ip Ownership Assignment (Support: 248)
     * Irrevocable Or Perpetual License (Support: 19)
     * License Grant (Support: 527)
     * Non-Disparagement (Support: 45)
     * Non-Transferable License (Support: 79)
     * Notice Period To Terminate Renewal (Support: 20)
     * Renewal Term (Support: 132)
     * Rofr/Rofo/Rofn (Support: 280)
     * Source Code Escrow (Support: 50)
   - Alternatively, we should focus on the strongly correlated ones but manually inject contrastive samples.

C. Do we have enough data to construct meaningful contextual examples?
   - No. The training support for many minority classes is extremely low (under 10 samples total), and they have zero risk-level variation.

D. What exact data-generation/augmentation strategy do you recommend?
   - We recommend contrastive adversarial data generation using LLMs. For each highly correlated clause type (e.g. Non-Compete), generate matched low-risk and medium-risk variations by adding qualifiers (e.g. 'non-compete only applies to a 1-mile radius' or 'non-compete is completely mutual and only applies during active service'). This breaks the correlation shortcut and forces the model to look at grammatical context.

E. Is Experiment 5 likely to be worthwhile?
   - Yes, but only if we use synthetic contrastive data augmentation to train the risk encoder. Training again on the original splits with different hyperparameters will not improve real-world risk generalization.
